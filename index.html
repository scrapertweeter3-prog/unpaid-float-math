The hours a gig shift shows up without pay

Take-home-per-hour looks different once the clock stops at the app and starts at the driver. A one-hour gig run does not pay for one hour. Rolling payout holds sit on the newest earnings for up to a week, and a deactivation appeal adds its own 72-hour response window before anyone reads the ticket. On the weeks those windows stack, the meter runs while the money does not.

Put numbers on it. A ten-hour week at a $22 effective rate keeps about $220 of the newest earnings in float at all times, and 8 percent on held cash prices that float at $18 a year. The heavier line is the lockout: a six-week deactivation at the same pace deletes 60 hours, $1,320 gross. Price both across a 1,000-hour gig year and the quoted $22 an hour settles near $20.66 once float and lockout risk are in. The rate a platform quotes is the rate before the platform's own windows act on it.

That math is on the site already: ExtraHustles price deactivation before it prices you, and the gig-app breakdowns there treat the true hourly rate as the only number worth quoting. Count the unpaid hours, not the shift hours.
